The decisions, in one page

One wall
Any internal user sees cost. No external view renders it — nor margin percent, discount depth, floor-price warnings, rate cards or ownership code. There is no internal tiering.
Not rendered
A restricted route, nav entry or field does not exist for that role. Nothing appears greyed out, and the interface is never the enforcement mechanism.
Gates decide first
License, release and AR gates determine availability before any screen renders. Every block states a reason and a path forward.
Released means two things
Orderable requires package status available and lab result TestPassed. Both. Nothing else makes a product appear in a catalog.
Tag is the lot key
The compliance tag identifies the lot externally. Batch number and lot ID are enrichment shown when present — they are absent on most inventory today.
Four states out, ten in
Stores see Placed, Approved, Shipped, Received. Operations keeps the full Canix set so the portal and Canix never disagree.

NOTE
Server-side authorization is required and is the actual control. Field-level filtering happens before a response leaves the server — including in search results, exports, notification payloads and error messages. Interface hiding is a usability measure, never the security mechanism. When an administrator views another user’s screen, cost is withheld even though that administrator could see it in their own view, so an impersonated screenshot can never carry cost out of the building.
06

Gates

Seven gates. Each states where it applies and what the blocked user sees, because a block without an explanation is the single largest generator of workarounds — the user calls a rep, and the portal has lost.

License expired at a location
Blocks ordering for that location only; other locations continue
“Ordering for this shop is paused — its license record expired on 12 Aug. Upload the renewal, or contact your account manager.” Catalog stays browsable. The upload has a home from rev 2.1 — section 20 — and landing a file does not lift this gate; an internal reviewer updating the license record does.
Not released
Availability — the SKU is not rendered at all
Nothing. An unreleased or failed lot has no external presence, so there is no state to explain. Internally it appears in the release queue with its quarantine reason.
AR past due
Fires at order entry, not at shipping
“This order can’t be submitted while the account has a past-due balance.” The buyer sees that the gate fired and who to contact; the balance itself is shown only to the owner.
Requires owner approval
On submit: buyer-submitted, over threshold, over available quantity, or AR past due
“Sent to [owner] for approval.” The draft stays visible and editable, the trigger is named, and the owner is notified. Where the owner has delegated approval — to a second person in their organisation or to their urbanXtracts rep — the notice names who holds it and until when. Section 16.
COA pending or amended
On the lot panel and the public resolver
Pending: “Testing in progress for this lot — results aren’t published yet.” No promise of a date, no interpretation. Amended: the current version is shown with an amendment marker, and accounts that bought the lot are notified.
Inventory changed after adding
On draft review and again on submit
“Available quantity for this line dropped to 18 since you added it.” The line is flagged, not silently reduced, and the buyer chooses.
Lot recalled or withdrawn
Catalog, open drafts, placed orders and the public resolver, at once
The lot stops being orderable immediately, and a draft line holding it is blocked rather than silently dropped. Accounts that received it see a notice on the order and on the lot, and their acknowledgement is recorded. The trigger and the wording are the CCO’s; the portal carries whatever is approved and never composes the message itself.

Notifications. Four events push to a store user: an order state change, an approaching license expiry, a past-due balance, and an amended COA or recall notice on a lot the account received. The fourth is new in rev 2 — section 10 already promised it and section 11 did not carry it, so the most safety-relevant event was the one event that did not push. Kiosk mode

A shared floor tablet has no individual user, which means it cannot carry attribution. Rather than pretend otherwise, kiosk mode is education-only: nothing it can do requires an audit trail. The device is authorised to a location by an urbanXtracts administrator, and revoking it is a single internal action rather than a request to the store.

Open items

Each item names the question, its class, whether it stops a gate, and which gate. Owners are proposed. Blocking means one thing in both documents: the item stops a screen, a gate or a publication from being correct, so nothing downstream of it can be called finished. A connection that has not been attached is not counted as a blocker — those are tracked in section 09. Ten of the twenty-nine items are blocking. This list is the register: the validation queue in the prototype renders the same twenty-nine items under the same classes, and the two are checked against each other. Nothing here is treated as decided unless it carries a decided marker.

Recall and lot impact

Lineage in rev 1 ran forward — inbound lot, batch, package, release, one allocation. A recall, a withdrawal and an amended COA all ask the opposite question, who has this, and nothing answered it. This section adds the reverse view and the notice that runs on top of it. The trigger and the wording stay with the CCO; what follows is mechanism only.

Input
A compliance tag, or any identifier that resolves to exactly one — never a partial.
Returns
Every account and location that received the lot, with quantity, order, delivery date and the person who signed for it.
Also returns
Quantity sitting in an open draft, quantity allocated to an unshipped order, and quantity still in urbanXtracts custody. A recall that only chases delivered stock misses the three places it is easiest to stop.
Never returns
One counterparty’s row to another, by any route. Ownership code stays internal, as everywhere else.
Records
Notice issued, channel, and acknowledgement per account with a timestamp. Acknowledgement is by account, never by named person — the store decides who answers for it.
Store sees
A notice on the affected order and on the lot, the approved wording, and one action to acknowledge. No internal owner, no clock, no lineage.
Safety escalates, it does not queue
A product-safety issue goes to the CCO immediately and is itself a recall trigger. It does not wait behind this view, and this view is not the escalation path — it is what the escalation uses once the CCO has decided.
15

Life after submission

Rev 1 defined the states well and the actions poorly: Placed promised edit, duplicate and cancel while no screen offered any of them, and a store receiving eighteen of twenty-four had exactly one route — the telephone, which is the failure the portal exists to remove.

Edit window
A placed order stays editable by the submitting organisation until it is approved. After approval a change is a request, not an edit, and routes to the assigned rep. Every edit writes a line-level diff both sides can read.
Cancel
The same window. After approval, cancellation is a request with a stated reason.
Receiving claim
At Received, and for a window still to be set, a store raises short, damaged or refused per line — quantity, reason, optional photo. It lands in the exception queue that already exists.
While a claim is open
The store sees the claim state on the order and nothing else. Never the internal owner, the clock, or internal notes — unchanged from the segregation rule in section 05.
Draft hold
Undecided, and stated as undecided. Today a draft holds nothing, which is precisely why the inventory-changed gate exists. The gate stays whichever way the policy lands; the policy is an open item.
16

Approval authority and delegation

One named human held every approval in rev 1, with no delegate and no clock — an owner on holiday stalls every order over the threshold. You have separately asked that reps be able to handle store approvals. Both are the same mechanism, a delegation, and the second carries a question the first does not.

Default
The store owner holds approval for their organisation. Unchanged.
Delegate inside the store
The owner grants to another named user in the same organisation, scoped by location, with an expiry date. Revocable in one action.
Delegate to the urbanXtracts rep
The same record, the same expiry, the same revocation — plus a standing written authorisation held on the account, not merely a click in a screen.
Named on the order
Wherever an approval appears, it names who holds the authority and until when. An order approved under delegation says so on its face, permanently.
Clock
An approval not actioned inside the set period escalates on a named path shown on screen. An order never expires quietly.
Never delegable
The license gate, the AR gate and the available-quantity gate. A delegate approves an order. A delegate does not lift a block, and no delegation can be configured to.
What rep-held approval changes
A rep approving a store’s order means the seller is approving the buyer’s purchase commitment. That is a segregation-of-duties question and a contracting question before it is a feature: who is committed, on what authority, and what the store’s written authorisation actually says. The design keeps it survivable — granted by the store, time-bounded, revocable instantly, named on the order, visible in the store’s own audit view, and unable to lift any gate. Whether it may exist at all is for sales leadership with the CCO and Legal, and it is carried as blocking in section 13.
17

Store account, users and records

The matrix in rev 1 granted the owner users, receivables and account settings. The build carried a role change and nothing else: no invite, no way to remove a departing buyer, no balance, no delivery settings. This is the store’s own administrative surface, and the window onto what urbanXtracts holds about them.

Users
Invite, activate, change role, deactivate, and last active. Deactivation is immediate and ends live sessions — revocation is a control, not a convenience. Kiosk devices stay urbanXtracts-paired: a device has no person to attribute anything to.
Account and delivery
Locations, the license record and its expiry, delivery windows, per-location receiving instructions and the receiving contact.
Receivables
Balance, terms, invoices, what is past due, and what the gate is reading. Owner only. A buyer still sees that the gate fired and nothing more.
Records
Orders and their lines; lots received with the COA version current at receipt; licenses and expiries; claims and their state; documents exchanged; notices issued and acknowledged; and the organisation’s own audit history. One place, no rep needed.
Export
Available on the records surface, and the export obeys exactly the field filtering the screen obeys — scope, row count and fields land in the audit trail.
Never here
Cost, margin, discount depth, ownership code, rate cards, or any part of another organisation. Unchanged, and unchanged by export.
18

Store data access — API and public resolver

Some chains run their own inventory or BI tooling and would rather pull than log in. Both halves of this section are places where data leaves with no person on the other end, which is why they share one set of rules.

Store API — read only
Scope
Exactly the records surface in section 17 — orders, lines, lots received, COA pointers, licenses, claims, notices. Nothing else has an endpoint.
One authorization
The API is a second renderer of the same authorization, never a second authorization. A field the screen withholds cannot be reached through it, including in errors and pagination metadata.
Credential
Issued and revoked by the store owner in the portal, scoped to that organisation’s licenses, rotatable on demand, with last-used shown.
Logged
Every read writes the same audit event as an export — scope, row count, fields.
No writes
An order is placed in the portal or by a rep. There is no path that reaches the order gates from outside them.
Limits
Rate limited and paginated. The numbers are an open item, not a detail to be discovered in production.
Public COA resolver
The gap
Rev 1 specified what the page displays and never specified that a third party could enumerate it. A walkable code space hands over a picture of batch and testing history to anyone patient.
Controls
A non-sequential code, a rate limit per source, no listing endpoint, no search, and a resolver that answers only to a code presented in full.
Indistinguishable
An unknown code and a real but unpublished code return the same response. The resolver never confirms that something exists.
Unchanged
What the page displays, how long a printed code keeps resolving, and whether retest history appears remain the CCO’s, exactly as recorded in section 13.

Measurement

The build plan’s definition of amazing is that a buyer reorders in under thirty seconds without calling a rep. That is a measurable claim, and nothing measures it. Instrumentation is cheap before launch and cannot be backfilled afterwards — without it, the ordering of wave 3 is judgement rather than evidence.

Does the hero path work?
Sign-in to submitted reorder — median and ninetieth percentile. If it is not under thirty seconds, wave 3 is building the wrong thing next.
Which gate costs most?
Blocks per gate, and what the user did in the ten minutes after. A gate that fires often and ends in a phone call is a policy problem, not a copy problem.
Is the catalog findable?
Search and filter sessions that end in an add. Separates a findability problem from a pricing problem.
Is the kiosk used?
Lookups per device per day, scanned against typed. Decides whether the device-framed pass earns its place.
Is the phone call gone?
Orders placed by the store against orders placed by a rep on their behalf. The single number that says whether any of this worked.
Never counted
Anything tying a budtender lookup to a consumer. The kiosk has no person behind it and gains none here.
20

Document exchange

The license gate has told a blocked store to “upload the renewal” since rev 1, and there has never been anywhere to upload it. This is that surface, and it runs in both directions: what a store sends us, and what we send back.

Who
Owner and buyer. A buyer’s documents are scoped to their own location; an owner sees the organisation’s. Budtender and kiosk cannot upload at all — a kiosk has no person behind it, so nothing it submitted could be attributed to anyone.
Typed, not a pile
Every upload picks from a controlled list — license renewal, insurance certificate, resale or exemption certificate, receiving instructions, claim evidence, signed confirmation. An untyped pile is unusable the moment it is more than ten files. The list itself is an open item.
What each carries
Type, the uploading user, timestamp, and an effective and expiry date wherever the type has one. Expiry drives a notice on the same path as license expiry.
Review state
Submitted, then accepted or rejected internally with a reason the store can read. A store is never left guessing whether a document landed.
Versions, not deletions
A store cannot delete an accepted document; it supersedes it with a newer one and both stay resolvable. Same treatment as a COA version, and for the same reason.
Outbound
Signed confirmations, notices and anything else we issue appear in the same place, so a store has one location for the paper between us rather than a mail search.
An upload never lifts a gate
A store uploading a license renewal does not clear its own license gate. The gate reads the license record; the record is updated by an internal reviewer who has looked at the document. Anything else would let a counterparty clear a compliance block by attaching a file. The same holds for insurance and exemption certificates.

Banking, remittance and signatory details are not accepted here. They live in the onboarding record and nowhere else. A store attempting to attach them is told where they belong rather than having them quietly accepted into general document storage.
21

Store onboarding, internally

Flow C has described organisation creation since rev 1 as a sequence of records with no screen behind it. This is the internal intake that makes it real — and it is internal-only: a store does not onboard itself, because two of its six stages are ours to decide and one of them is a compliance qualification.

01 · Intake
Legal entity, trading names, locations, and a license number per location — the license number is the account key, so a location without one cannot proceed. Contacts and their roles. Sales and account management.
02 · Qualification
Each license checked against the state record and its expiry captured. We record what the state record says; we never assert a license is valid on our own authority. A location that fails qualification does not become orderable, and the account can still proceed without it. Quality and compliance.
03 · Commercial terms
Rate card assignment, payment terms, credit limit, and the basis the AR gate will read. Blocked until the per-account rate card exists — the wave 1 blocker reaches this far. Finance, with sales leadership.
04 · Account creation
Organisation and locations created, account key set, delivery windows and receiving contact captured. Administrator.
05 · Access
The owner is invited, and from that moment administers their own users under section 17. Kiosk devices are paired by us, not by them. Administrator.
06 · Ready to order
All gates evaluated for the first time. An account reaching this stage can place an order; an account short of it is visible internally with exactly which stage it is sitting in. Sales and account management.
What the onboarding record holds, and keeps
License numbers, banking, remittance and signatory details are captured here and stay here. They are not surfaced anywhere else in the portal afterwards — not on the account screen, not in the store records surface, not through the store API, and not in an export. The account key that the rest of the system uses is the license number; everything else in this paragraph has no second home by design.

Acceptance

Criteria
Every visibility derives from a stated rule. Every gate has a user-visible reason and a path forward. Every screen element names its source. No view can render another organisation’s data by any route, including search, export, notification payloads and error messages.
Definition of done
Phase 1 closes when the matrix, gates and flows are approved and every blocking open item is either answered or explicitly accepted as open into Phase 2. Approval of this document is not approval to build the economics view.
Required tests
An expired license blocks ordering at that location only. Unreleased product is not visible. The AR gate fires at order entry, not at shipping. One store cannot see another’s orders. A blocked user is given a path forward. Cost is absent from every external view and from every impersonated view. Added in rev 2: a recalled lot leaves every catalog and blocks every draft holding it. A delegated approval cannot lift a gate. A deactivated user loses a live session. An API credential cannot return a field the screen withholds. An unknown COA code and an unpublished one are indistinguishable.
Evidence & owner
Evidence: this document, plus the connector reads dated 2026-08-24 behind each verified field. Owner: whoever holds portal delivery. Approver: to be named — recorded as proposed.
Every control described here is designed. None is live, and none should be described as compliant or sufficient. Structural facts were read from live connectors; all product, price, cost, order and COA values are synthetic.

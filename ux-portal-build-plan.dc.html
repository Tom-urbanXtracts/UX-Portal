Wave 1

Unblock the hero flow

Nothing else matters as much as this: catalog to product to lot to order, with a price a buyer can trust. Three items, none of them mine. Blocking means the same thing here and in the architecture: it stops a screen, a gate or a publication from being correct. Ten items are blocking. A connection that has not been attached is tracked separately and is not counted as one.

Build the per-account rate card
A record of SKU by account with price, effective date and an approver. Today price is derived from the last qualifying order line, which is a stopgap that quotes nothing for a new account or a new SKU. This single record also retires the price-on-request path, the price-changed warning and half the pricing caveats in the interface.

The honest summary
The portal already proves its architecture: role separation holds, gates block with reasons, orders move through their whole lifecycle, and the interface refuses to invent a number it does not have. What stands between this and amazing is not more screens — it is a rate card, a potency record, and the decisions in wave 2. Wave 3 makes it feel finished; waves 1 and 2 make it true. Wave 4 is what a review of the build against its own architecture turned up: a lineage that could not answer who has this lot, a safety notification that was promised in one section and absent from another, an owner who could not remove a departing buyer, and a store with no way to report a short delivery except the telephone. Rep-held approval is the only item that is genuinely new rather than a gap, and it is the only one that needs Legal.

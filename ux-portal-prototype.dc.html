What a delegate can never do

A delegate approves an order. A delegate does not lift a block, and no delegation can be configured to.

License gate
A location whose licence has lapsed cannot be ordered for, whoever holds approval. Northgate is the live example.
Receivables gate
A past-due balance blocks submission at order entry. Approval authority does not reach it, and neither does the rep.
Available quantity
An order above what exists is not approvable into existence.

The clock

How long an approval may sit
Not set. How long, and where it escalates, is an open decision with sales leadership. Until it is answered an order can wait indefinitely, which is the failure the clock exists to prevent.
What an order will show
Whoever holds authority and until when, named on the order itself and kept there permanently. An order approved under a delegation says so on its face for the rest of its life.
When authority is withdrawn
It takes effect at once. An approval already in flight still names the person who held it — the record does not rewrite itself.

What the gate is reading

When it fires
At order entry, never at shipping. A blocked draft is kept exactly as it is — nothing is discarded and nothing is silently reduced.
What a buyer sees
That the gate fired, and who to contact. Not the balance, not the invoice, not the age. That distinction is the whole reason this screen is owner-only.
Scope
The location, not the organisation. Riverside being blocked does not stop Downtown ordering — and this column reports the receivables gate alone. A location can be clear here and still be unable to order, because another gate is holding it.
The threshold
Undefined. Whether past due means a number of days, an amount, or both has not been decided, so today the flag is set by hand rather than computed. It is carried as a blocking item in the validation queue, owned by finance.
The source
The ledger, once connected. Until then every figure here is a placeholder and the portal says so rather than estimating one.
